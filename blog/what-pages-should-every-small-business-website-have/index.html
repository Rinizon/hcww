What Pages Should Every Small-Business Website Have?

Published August 3, 2026

A small-business website does not need dozens of pages to be effective, but it does need the right ones. Clear page structure helps visitors understand what the business does, trust it faster, and take the next step without confusion.

Many underperforming websites have the same problem: important information is missing, buried, or spread across pages that do not have a clear job. A simpler, better-planned structure usually works better than a larger, messier one.

Home page

The home page should quickly explain who the business serves, what it offers, and what a visitor should do next. It is not supposed to answer every possible question, but it should make the overall direction of the business obvious within a few seconds.

Service pages

Each major service should have its own page whenever possible. Separate service pages give customers clearer information and help search engines connect the site to specific searches. A business that offers web design, managed IT, and consulting should not try to force all of that into one vague paragraph.

About page

People want to know who they are dealing with. An about page helps explain the business, its approach, and why someone should trust it. For smaller local businesses, this page can do a lot of credibility work that a generic home page cannot.

Contact page

Every website should make it easy to reach the business. A contact page should include the preferred contact method, service area details when relevant, and a straightforward path for the visitor to start the conversation.

Pricing or process page

Not every business can post exact prices, but most can explain how projects are scoped or what factors influence cost. A pricing or process page helps qualify leads and reduces the friction that comes from uncertainty.

Proof and trust-building content

Depending on the business, this might be testimonials, case studies, certifications, FAQs, or practical blog content. Visitors often need reassurance before they contact a company, especially if the service is technical, specialized, or a meaningful investment.

What to avoid

Avoid pages that exist only because they seem standard. Thin pages with little useful information can dilute the site and make navigation less clear. It is better to have a smaller set of strong pages than a large set of weak ones.

Why structure matters for SEO

Good page structure does more than help people browse. It also gives search engines a clearer picture of what the business does and which pages are relevant for specific searches. When service pages, supporting content, and contact paths are organized well, the site becomes easier to understand for both visitors and Google.
