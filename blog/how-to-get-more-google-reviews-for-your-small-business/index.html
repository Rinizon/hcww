How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Small Business

Published November 9, 2026

Google reviews help prospective customers understand what it is like to work with your business. A simple, respectful review process can turn good service into useful local proof without pressuring customers or cutting corners.

For many local businesses, a Google review is one of the first signals a potential customer sees. Reviews can strengthen trust, answer common questions, and give your Google Business Profile fresh, customer-written context. They are not a shortcut to strong service, but a dependable process for asking can ensure satisfied customers have an easy chance to share their experience.

Make sure your Google Business Profile is ready

Before asking for reviews, confirm that your business name, phone number, website, hours, service areas, and primary category are accurate. Use a profile your business controls, with more than one trusted owner where appropriate. Customers should be able to find the right listing without confusion. Our guide to Google Business Profiles covers the basics of keeping that important local listing complete.

Ask at the right moment

The best time to ask is soon after a customer has received the value they hired you for: after a project is complete, a service issue is resolved, an order is delivered, or a follow-up confirms they are happy. Do not wait so long that the experience is hard to remember. A brief, genuine request works better than a generic campaign sent months later.

Make leaving a review easy

Reduce the task to one clear action. Share your direct Google review link in a thank-you email, text message, invoice follow-up, or printed handout. Put the link behind a plain label such as “Leave us a Google review,” rather than asking customers to search for your business themselves. Test the link periodically on a phone and computer so it opens the correct listing.

Use a simple, honest request

Your request does not need elaborate wording. Thank the customer, say that feedback helps local people choose a provider, and include the link. For example: “Thanks for choosing us. If you have a minute, would you share your experience in a Google review? Your feedback helps other local customers find us.” Keep the choice voluntary and avoid telling customers what rating or language to use.

Do not offer incentives or filter feedback

Avoid discounts, gifts, contests, or other rewards in exchange for reviews. Do not ask only customers you expect to leave positive feedback while diverting unhappy customers elsewhere. These practices can undermine trust and may conflict with platform rules. Ask a broad, reasonable group of real customers for an honest review, then use private conversations to resolve service concerns when they arise.

Build review requests into your normal workflow

Consistency matters more than a large one-time push. Choose a natural point in your customer process, decide who sends the request, and use a short template that staff can personalize. A weekly or monthly check-in can show whether requests are going out and whether the review link still works. Document the process so it continues when roles or staff change.

Respond to positive reviews with care

A timely thank-you shows that the business is attentive. Keep the response warm and specific without revealing personal details about the customer or their project. You can mention the service provided in general terms and invite them back. Avoid copying the same response word-for-word; a short, real reply is more helpful than a polished template that sounds automated.

Handle negative reviews professionally

Read a critical review carefully before replying. Thank the reviewer for the feedback, acknowledge the concern when appropriate, and offer a direct way to continue the conversation offline. Do not argue, disclose account details, or make promises you cannot keep. A calm public response can reassure future customers that the business takes problems seriously. If a review appears fraudulent or violates Google’s policies, document the issue and use the reporting process rather than escalating publicly.

Learn from recurring feedback

Reviews often reveal patterns that internal teams miss. Look for repeated comments about responsiveness, scheduling, communication, pricing clarity, or product quality. Share useful themes with the people who can improve the experience. The goal is not simply to increase a star rating; it is to make the business more reliable and easier to work with over time.
