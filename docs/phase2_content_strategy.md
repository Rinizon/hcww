# Phase 2 Content Strategy

This document turns the Phase 2 goals in `docs/phases.md` into a concrete content plan for the current site build.

It is the working source of truth for:

- page section order
- core message priorities
- trust themes to repeat across pages
- call to action intent for each page
- voice and tone guardrails during implementation

## 1. Phase 2 Goal

Phase 2 should make the site feel intentionally branded and clearly positioned, not merely scaffolded.

By the end of this phase, visitors should quickly understand:

- Hill Country Web Works serves small businesses in and around Boerne
- the company combines website services, managed IT support, and technical consulting
- Robert Kane brings enterprise experience from Microsoft and Rackspace
- the experience of working together is direct, calm, clear, and reliable
- the next step is a conversation, not a confusing sales funnel

## 2. Primary Audience

The main audience for the first site version is local small-business owners and operators who:

- need a modern website
- have recurring IT problems or weak support
- want one trusted local partner instead of multiple vendors
- are not deeply technical and want plain-English guidance

Secondary audiences:

- organizations that already have a website but need maintenance, hosting, or troubleshooting
- businesses with cloud, email, DNS, or server issues that need senior technical help

## 3. Messaging Priorities

Every Phase 2 page should reinforce these ideas in roughly this order:

1. Local trust: Hill Country service, Boerne roots, direct relationship.
2. Clear outcome: better websites, more dependable support, less stress.
3. Enterprise credibility: Microsoft and Rackspace background.
4. Simplicity: plain-English communication and practical recommendations.
5. Long-term partnership: ongoing support, maintenance, and follow-through.

## 4. Voice and Tone Guardrails

Use copy that is:

- friendly
- direct
- calm
- capable
- clear for non-technical readers

Avoid copy that is:

- jargon-heavy
- dramatic or alarmist
- overly polished in a corporate way
- vague about services or next steps

Preferred writing patterns:

- short paragraphs
- concrete service language
- reassuring statements about communication and reliability
- direct calls to action centered on consultation or conversation

## 5. Reusable Trust Themes

These themes should appear repeatedly across the homepage and the four Phase 2 supporting pages.

### Local and Personal

- Boerne-based
- serves the Texas Hill Country
- clients work directly with the founder

### Enterprise Background

- former Microsoft Senior Support Escalation Engineer
- Rackspace experience
- advanced troubleshooting depth without enterprise complexity for the client

### Reliable Working Style

- clear communication
- documented next steps
- permanent fixes over quick patches
- dependable follow-through

### Plain-English Support

- technical topics explained simply
- practical recommendations
- friendly guidance for non-technical business owners

## 6. Conversion Strategy

The primary conversion across Phase 2 pages is a consultation-oriented contact action.

Primary CTA language:

- Get Started
- Schedule a Consultation
- Talk About Your Project
- Request an Estimate

Secondary CTA language:

- View Services
- Compare Options
- Learn How We Work

Conversion intent by page:

- Homepage: move first-time visitors toward services or contact
- About: turn credibility into trust and conversation
- Services: help visitors identify their service category and ask questions
- Pricing: reduce uncertainty and encourage estimate requests
- Contact: make outreach feel easy and low-pressure

## 7. Homepage Content Outline

### Purpose

Create immediate clarity, establish trust quickly, and guide visitors toward the two main next steps: view services or start a conversation.

### Section Order

1. Hero
2. Quick trust snapshot
3. Services overview
4. Why clients choose HCWW
5. Local service area and relationship model
6. Final CTA

### Key Message by Section

#### Hero

- Headline should position HCWW as the local source for modern websites and reliable tech support.
- Subheadline should combine enterprise skill with approachable local service.
- Primary CTA should lead to contact.
- Secondary CTA should lead to services.

#### Quick trust snapshot

- Introduce Robert Kane by name.
- Mention Microsoft and Rackspace early.
- Reinforce direct communication and small-business focus.

#### Services overview

- Present the three service pillars:
  - website design and hosting
  - managed IT support
  - cloud and technical consulting
- Explain each in plain language with outcome-oriented summaries.

#### Why clients choose HCWW

- Focus on communication, reliability, technical depth, and practical recommendations.
- Emphasize that clients get one partner who can handle both web and technical needs.

#### Local service area and relationship model

- Reassure visitors that HCWW serves Boerne and surrounding Hill Country communities.
- Frame the business as relationship-driven, not ticket-queue driven.

#### Final CTA

- Offer a free consultation or practical first conversation.
- Make the next step feel approachable and low pressure.

## 8. About Page Content Outline

### Purpose

Build trust by showing who Robert is, how the business was shaped, and what clients can expect from the relationship.

### Section Order

1. Intro and positioning
2. Founder story
3. Mission, values, and local commitment
4. Differentiators and working style
5. Closing CTA

### Key Message by Section

#### Intro and positioning

- State that HCWW is built on enterprise experience and local service.
- Signal the page is about trust, not a generic company history.

#### Founder story

- Trace the arc from Microsoft to Rackspace to serving Hill Country businesses.
- Present the move as a deliberate shift toward direct, dependable community-focused work.

#### Mission, values, and local commitment

- Use reliability, integrity, excellence, community, and simplicity as the core values framework.
- Reinforce the mission to make technology manageable for small businesses.

#### Differentiators and working style

- Explain direct communication, documentation, and calm problem-solving.
- Make it clear clients are not buying anonymous support.

#### Closing CTA

- Invite readers to talk through a website, support need, or technical problem.

## 9. Services Page Content Outline

### Purpose

Give visitors a clear top-level understanding of the three service categories and help them identify where they fit.

### Section Order

1. Intro and umbrella positioning
2. Website design and hosting summary
3. Managed IT support summary
4. Cloud and technical consulting summary
5. How to choose the right starting point
6. Closing CTA

### Key Message by Section

#### Intro and umbrella positioning

- Lead with the hybrid model: websites plus tech support in one place.
- Explain that clients do not need to know exactly which bucket their problem fits into before reaching out.

#### Service summaries

- Website design and hosting: modern sites, maintenance, security, performance, and updates.
- Managed IT support: backups, troubleshooting, monitoring, email, cloud fixes, and light device support.
- Cloud and technical consulting: AWS, Azure, Linux, DNS, email, security, and urgent troubleshooting.

#### How to choose the right starting point

- Reduce friction by telling visitors HCWW can help clarify scope during an initial conversation.

#### Closing CTA

- Encourage a consultation for service selection or problem scoping.

## 10. Pricing Page Content Outline

### Purpose

Provide enough pricing detail to build trust and help visitors self-qualify without turning the page into a rigid rate card.

### Section Order

1. Intro and pricing philosophy
2. Website project ranges
3. Hosting and maintenance ranges
4. Managed IT retainer ranges
5. Hourly and emergency rates
6. What every engagement includes
7. Closing CTA

### Key Message by Section

#### Intro and pricing philosophy

- Emphasize transparency, scope alignment, and clear communication.
- Explain that ranges help with planning and that final pricing follows a conversation.

#### Price sections

- Website projects: basic, standard, and e-commerce add-on framing.
- Hosting and maintenance: monthly support range.
- Managed IT: Bronze, Silver, and Gold retainer framing.
- Hourly and emergency: standard and urgent-response ranges.

#### What every engagement includes

- Clear communication
- documented next steps
- practical recommendations
- honest scoping

#### Closing CTA

- Invite visitors to request an estimate or discuss the right service level.

## 11. Contact Page Content Outline

### Purpose

Make contacting HCWW feel simple, welcoming, and low risk.

### Section Order

1. Intro and reassurance
2. Contact options
3. What to send or ask about
4. Response expectation
5. Contact form

### Key Message by Section

#### Intro and reassurance

- Encourage visitors to reach out whether they need a new website, recurring IT help, or urgent troubleshooting.
- Remove pressure by framing the first touchpoint as a conversation.

#### Contact options

- Phone
- email
- business hours
- optional future scheduling link when available

#### What to send or ask about

- business type
- project or support need
- timeline
- current pain point

#### Response expectation

- Set expectations for clear follow-up and practical next steps.

## 12. Implementation Notes for Phase 2 Build

Use this outline to guide page rewrites before adding deeper Phase 3 sections.

Important implementation decisions:

- Homepage should carry the heaviest trust-building burden.
- About should deepen the founder story, not repeat the homepage verbatim.
- Services and Pricing should stay plain-English even when mentioning technical capability.
- Contact should reduce hesitation and avoid sounding like a formal ticket intake form.
